**Status:** encoded and tested; **awaiting review against the statute** (roadmap Phase 4).

Lean proves that each explanation agrees with its rule, and that the conclusions follow from the definitions.
It cannot prove that the definitions match the Act. That is what this review is for.

## Interpretation choices to confirm

1. **The period includes both ends.** "Begins 30 days before and ends 30 days after" is read as day −30 to day +30 inclusive (61 days), at the granularity of whole days.
2. **"Within 30 days after" in (f) and (g)** is read as the day of the disposition through day +30. An event on the day of the disposition counts. An event before it does not.
3. **"Identical" is a judgment call** for each acquisition, with a risk for each answer. `same` (the very property) and the deeming rules in (i) and (j) are treated as facts.
4. **Paragraph (i)** is encoded for a right to acquire *the particular property*. A right to acquire a property that is itself only *identical* to it would need the identity judgment as well. It is not modelled separately.
5. **Paragraph (j)** is encoded as the substituted property being a share of a SIFT wind-up corporation in respect of the entity whose equity is the particular property. "Acquired before 2013" means before 2013-01-01.
6. **Paragraph (b)** ("owns or had a right to acquire") is a single fact per acquisition. It isn't derived from later dispositions.
7. **Inputs from provisions not yet encoded** are taken as facts:
   - affiliation under paragraphs 251.1(1)(b) to (g), and who is a spouse or common-law partner;
   - loss restriction event (s. 251.2);
   - whether 40(2)(e.1), 40(3.4) or 69(5) applies;
   - the 40(2)(g) foreign affiliate carve-out.
8. **Any one qualifying acquisition is enough.** When several qualify, the explanation cites one that rests on facts before one that rests on judgment.
9. **"To the extent that" is not quantified.** The rule decides whether the loss is a superficial loss. `deniedLoss` treats the whole loss as denied. Partial cases (for example, fewer shares bought back than sold) are left for a later phase. The Act gives no formula for them.
10. **Not encoded:** subsection 50(2) (which debts it refers to), section 251 (arm's length), and the amount in 53(1)(f)(ii); each is an input.
11. **53(1)(f), amount (i):** "the amount of the loss that was, because of the acquisition by the taxpayer of the property, a superficial loss" is an input. In the spouse transaction it is the whole denied loss, which assumes the spouse's shares are the only substituted property.
12. **53(1)(f), amount (ii):** the amount that would be deducted under 112(3) to (3.2) is an input (nil in the examples). "The amount, if any, by which (i) exceeds (ii)" is read as (i) minus (ii), or nil.
13. **251.1(1)(a)** is read as requiring the first person to be an individual and the second to be that individual's spouse or common-law partner. It is symmetric in practice, but the encoding takes the facts in that order.
14. **Affiliation can be a judgment call.** Whether one of paragraphs 251.1(1)(b) to (g) applies is a fact or a judgment call. When it is a judgment call, the superficial loss acquirer records it (`Acquirer.judged`), and every finding that turns on it relies on it.
15. **40(2)(g)(ii):** "acquired … for the purpose of gaining or producing income" and arm's length dealing are each a fact or a judgment call. The "unless" is read as two alternative exceptions. Exempt income is not separately modelled: the income purpose must be "other than exempt income".
16. **"Personal-use property" says "includes".** Its three listed categories are encoded. Any ordinary meaning beyond them must come in through the judgment call in paragraph (a). The partnership part of the definition is not encoded.
17. **"Listed personal property"** is read as personal-use property that is, or is a portion of or an interest in or right to, one of the five listed kinds. Which kind it is, if any, is a fact.
18. **40(2)(g)(iv):** the TFSA holder is treated as "a beneficiary" under the TFSA. "Within 60 days after the end of the taxation year" in (B) is a single fact, not computed from dates.
19. **Section 251.1 is computed for a world of persons** as the smallest relation that contains every pair paragraphs (a) to (g) name and is symmetric and reflexive. The recursive references ("is affiliated with" inside (c), (d), (f) and (g), and in the majority-interest tests) are read as references to that same relation. (g)(ii) uses the relation computed without paragraph (g). Each result is checked to be closed.
20. **"Controlled" means "directly or indirectly in any manner whatever"** (251.1(3)). Each control is given as a fact or a judgment call (for example, de facto control). Indirect control must be given explicitly: the encoding does not infer that a parent's controller controls its subsidiary.
21. **Control by "a person" and by "a group of persons" are kept apart,** as (b) and (c) distinguish them. For (d), "controlled by a particular group of persons" is read as a group; to treat one person as a group of one, give the control as a group with one member.
22. **Majority-interest partner (248(1)):** the share the taxpayer "would" have had, holding every interest it or an affiliate holds, is taken as the sum of those interests' shares. A majority-interest group of partners is computed from all subsets of the partners: the group's combined shares exceed half, and no member can be dropped. With this reading, 251.1(1)(f)(ii) is always also reached through (f)(i).
23. **Majority-interest beneficiary (251.1(3)):** interests are given at fair market value, after applying 251.1(4)(d)(i) (discretionary powers deemed exercised or not). A trust needs its own interest of the kind in question (251.1(4)(d)(iii)). 251.1(4)(d)(ii) (arm's length) is not needed for (a) to (g).
24. **Paragraph (h):** whether a person is a "contributor" (251.1(3), with its arm's length and fair value exceptions) is a fact, and so is who is "connected by blood relationship, marriage, common-law partnership or adoption" (251.1(4)(d)(iv)). (g)(ii) reads the subsection without (g), so it includes (h).
25. **Not encoded:** 251.1(4)(d)(ii) (arm's length with a trust).
26. **"Controls" in 251(2) is read as de jure control** (control of the votes), since 251(2) does not say "directly or indirectly in any manner whatever". A control can be marked de facto only: it then counts for affiliation, not for 251(2). Rights under 251(5)(b), and 251(5)(a) (a related group in a position to control is deemed to control), must be reflected in the controls given.
27. **A person is not related to itself,** except that 251(5)(c) makes a shareholder of two corporations related to itself in each capacity: the encoding lets one person satisfy "related to" in 251(2)(c) for that reason.
28. **251(6)(a) "brother or sister"** is computed as sharing a parent (so half-siblings count), or given directly. Lineal descent is through children by blood; adoptions count only through 251(6)(c).
29. **251(6)(c)** is read as: one adopted, legally or in fact, as the child of the other, or as the child of a person who is the other's ancestor or descendant (the words "otherwise than as a brother or sister" exclude siblings).
30. **251(1)(b)** (personal trusts) is a fact, not computed: the 248(25) reference is not encoded.
31. **Not encoded:** the 125(7) purposes of 251(5).
32. **40(3.3) to (3.5):** "owns" in (3.3)(c) is a fact per acquisition (unlike the superficial loss definition, a right to acquire does not count there). Each release time in 40(3.4)(b)(i) to (v) is given as the date it occurs; (i) is the day the 30-day period without the property begins. The deeming rules in 40(3.5)(c) and (d) (mergers, wind-ups, redemptions) must be reflected in who owns the property. The partnership continuation rule at the end of 40(3.4) is not encoded.
33. **Amalgamations (251.1(2), 251(3.1)).** The supposition is a hypothetical world: the world immediately before, with the new corporation added and controlled as it is immediately after, by the shareholders it then has (the control must be given, as elsewhere). The predecessors keep their own shareholders in that world. "Deemed to have been affiliated [related]" is reported as a yes-or-no answer; the times for which the deeming matters (for example, under 40(3.4)) are for the provision that uses it.
34. **251(3.2).** Control that exists only because of a 251(5)(b) right is left out by giving the world without it (`beforeWithoutRights`). "Each of which was related … to each other" is read as every pair of predecessors being related. The deemed relationship is reported between the new corporation and each predecessor.
35. **Not encoded:** 40(3.5)(c), which deems the corporation formed on a merger to own a share whose loss is suspended.
36. **50(2):** "established by the taxpayer to have become a bad debt in the year" is a fact or a judgment call, and so is arm's length (which 251 can supply). "The amount, if any, by which (i) exceeds (ii)" is (i) minus (ii), or nil. The resulting loss is proven to be the lesser of the debt's cost and the gain.
37. **"To the extent that" for (ii) to (iv)** is read as the whole loss: the loss is entirely from the disposition of the debt, personal-use property, or property transferred. `Paragraph40_2_g.deniedLoss` treats the whole loss as nil when any subparagraph applies, as `LossDenial.deniedLoss` does for (i).
38. **Capital gains (38 to 41).** Taxable amounts are kept in half-cents, so ½ of an odd number of cents is exact (shown as, for example, $254.995). Whether a gain or loss is on capital account (39(1)(a), (b): "would not … be included in computing the taxpayer's income") is a fact or a judgment call. 39(1)(c) (business investment loss) is a fact with its reductions given; "small business corporation" and 39(9), (10) are not encoded. 38(a.2) and (a.3) are not encoded. The 40(1)(a)(iii) reserve is the amount claimed, capped at the lesser of a reasonable reserve and ⅕ of the gain times (4 − preceding years), with the product rounded down to the cent; 40(1.1) (ten-year reserves) is not encoded.
39. **Section 41.** "Gains" and "losses" are 40(1) gains and losses. Listed-personal-property losses of other years are a single amount claimed; the ordering rules in 41(2)(b)(i), (ii) are for the taxpayer to respect. 39(1)(a)(i.1) property is excluded from the pool's gains, as an excluded kind.
40. **Section 3.** Income from sources, Subdivision E deductions and losses from sources are given amounts. Unused allowable capital losses are reported, but the net capital loss carryover (111(1)(b), 111(8)) is not encoded.
41. **Net capital losses (111).** Each loss year's section 38 fraction is given (½ unless stated) rather than taken from a table of past rates, since the Act's current text states only ½. A × B / C is rounded down to the half-cent where it is not exact. The C term of 111(8) takes its three amounts as given. 111(1.1)(b) (pre-1986 losses) and (c) are not encoded. Taxable income (2(2)) is income less the net capital loss deduction and any other Division C deductions, given.
42. **40(3.6).** Affiliation is decided on the world immediately after the disposition. The share's class and fair market values of the remaining shares are given; each share's addition is rounded down to the cent, so the additions can fall short of the loss by a few cents (proven never to exceed it). "Distress preferred share" (80(1)) is a fact.
43. **40(2)(e.1).** "Related to each other" is read as each of the three pairs being related. Whether they would be related if 80(2)(j) applied is a fact. For 53(1)(f.1), whether both are taxable Canadian corporations, and whether 53(1)(f.2) applies, are facts.
44. **Principal residence.** Encoded for an individual other than a personal trust; (a.1), (c.1), 40(2)(c) and section 54.1 are not encoded. "Ordinarily inhabited" is a fact or a judgment call. Each other designation for a year is given with who made it; marital status and age are given per year. "Taxation years that end after the acquisition date" are the years listed, and each says whether the property was owned. A × B / C is rounded down to the cent, so any rounding is against the taxpayer by under a cent. D (the 1994 election) is given.
45. **69(1).** Arm's length is an `Answer`, from section 251 where it settles it. Fair market value is given. "Except as expressly otherwise provided" is not modelled: where a rollover (70(6), 73(1), 85(1) and so on) applies, 69(1) should not be applied to the same transfer. "Anything" is read as any property.
46. **73(1).** The kind of transferee in 73(1.01) is a fact, including whether a trust meets (c)(i), (ii) or (iii); 73(1.02) is not encoded. For depreciable property of a prescribed class, the proportion of undepreciated capital cost is given. Where 73(1) does not apply (including on an election out), the transfer is left to 69(1). The attribution rules (74.1, 74.2) are not encoded.
47. **Death (70(5), (6)).** The recipient, residence, whether the trust meets 70(6)(b)(i) and (ii), and vesting within 36 months are facts; the Minister's extension of that period is not modelled. For depreciable property the 70(6)(d)(i) amount is given; 70(5)(c), (d) and 70(6)(d.1), (e) are not encoded. 70(6.2) is a fact per property. Deemed dispositions on death carry `deemedBy := some .s70` for the superficial loss definition.
48. **Attribution (74.2, 74.5).** Which of the recipient's dispositions are of lent or transferred property (or property substituted for it) is given, as is whether each falls in the attribution period. "Directly or indirectly, by means of a trust or by any other means whatever" is part of what is given. 74.5(1)(b)'s two interest conditions are facts. The individual's attributed amounts enter their year through `Year.attribution`; the recipient's year leaves those dispositions out (74.2(1)(e)). 74.1 (income attribution), 74.3 and 74.4 are not encoded.
49. **Change in use (45).** Use is measured in parts of a whole, given before and after. A change from wholly one purpose to wholly the other is paragraph (a); any other change is (c). The deemed disposition is read as of the proportion of the property that changed, with that proportion of the adjusted cost base (45(1)(b)) as its cost; both amounts are rounded down to the cent. Whether the part that changes is personal-use property (so that a loss is nil under 40(2)(g)(iii)) is a fact. 45(2) is read as reaching (a)(i) and (c)(ii) and 45(3) as reaching (a)(ii) and (c)(i); 45(4) is a fact. Section 13, 45(1)(d), the timing of the 45(3) election, and the deemed change on rescission (45(2)(c)) are not encoded. For the principal residence definition, (d) counts preceding years that are owned, covered by an election, designated and not blocked, and not ordinarily inhabited.
50. **Transfer to a corporation (85(1), (2.1)).** Eligible property, a taxable Canadian corporation and a joint election filed in time are facts. (b), (c), (c.1), (e) and (e.3) are read together as: the elected amount is the amount agreed, raised to the boot and to the floor in (c.1) or (e), and capped at fair market value (proven). For capital property the cost amount is its adjusted cost base; for depreciable property, the class's undepreciated capital cost is given. Whether (e.2) applies is a fact or a judgment call, relied on only when there is an excess; the part of the excess is all of it unless given. Proportions in (f), (g), (h) and 85(2.1) are rounded down to the cent. Where 85(1) does not apply, the disposition is taken at fair market value. The paid-up capital increase for each class (before 85(2.1)) is given. (c.2), (e.1), (e.4), (e.5), (i), (1.11), (1.12), (1.2), 85(2.1)(b) and section 84.1 are not encoded; nor whether a loss on the transfer is denied (40(2)(g)(i), 40(3.4)), which those provisions decide.
51. **Section 84.1.** "Connected" (186(4)) is a fact, as are (2)(b), (2)(d) and whether (2.31) or (2.32) applies for (2)(e); otherwise arm's length is a fact or a judgment call. The (2)(a.1) reduction is given as one amount; 84.1(2)(a) (shares acquired before 1972) is not encoded. The new shares' paid-up capital increase per class is given. The proceeds otherwise are given (for a 85(1) transfer, the elected amount, via `ofTransfer`), and the dividend is taken out of them under 54 "proceeds of disposition" (k). A capital loss that results (for example, where the cost is mostly crystallized gains) is for 40(2)(g)(i) and 53(1)(f) to deal with. The dividend's gross-up and credit are not encoded.
52. **Capital gains deduction (110.6).** The deduction is in half-cents, like the taxable capital gains it shelters. The indexed 110.6(2)(a) amount is given ($625,000 by default, for years beginning before 2026), and E is 1. A + B + C + D (earlier deductions with their pre-2000 weights) is given, separately from the unweighted total for "cumulative gains limit" (d); so are earlier years' A and B, the 1985 3(e) amount, the cumulative net investment loss, the net capital losses deducted under 111(1)(b) for the year, and the 110.6(2) deduction. Which dispositions are of qualified small business corporation shares (or of qualified farm or fishing property) is given; `qualifiedShare` decides the definition for one share. "Small business corporation" (248(1)) is a fact or a judgment call. "More than 50%" is read strictly. (d) and (f) of the definition, 110.6(2.2), (2.3), (7) to (11), and the alternative minimum tax are not encoded.
53. **Loss restriction events (251.2, 256(7)).** Only corporations (251.2(2)(a)); trusts are not encoded. Each event is one acquisition, redemption or death, described by the world immediately before and after. Control is de jure (a control given as `deFactoOnly` does not count) and is acquired when a person or group controls after who did not before; a change in a controlling group's membership is an acquisition by the new group. Relatedness is section 251 on the world before, with rights under 251(5)(b) reflected in the controls given. In (i)(D) whether the deceased was related to the acquirer before the death is a fact. 256(7)(a)(i)(E), (ii)(B), 256(7)(b) to (g), 256(8) and 111(4) to (5.5) are not encoded. `eventDate` supplies the date to superficial loss (f); 40(3.4)(b)(iii) and the C term of 111(8) take it the same way.
54. **Income attribution (74.1).** The recipient's kind is a fact: a spouse or common-law partner (including one who has since become so), a minor who is not at arm's length with the individual or is a niece or nephew, or other. Which amounts are income or loss from the property or substituted property, and whether each falls in the period, are given; income on attributed income is not from the property and stays with the recipient. 74.5(1) is the existing `fairMarketValueException`, with `toSpouse` taken from the recipient. In 74.1(3), the fair market value used is capped at the other property's cost and the share is rounded down to the cent. The pension assignment exclusion in 74.1(1), 74.3, 74.4 and 56(4.1) are not encoded.
55. **40(3.5)(c), (d).** What happened to the share (a merger, an 88(1) winding-up, or a redemption, acquisition or cancellation by its issuer), its date, and the date the deemed owner ceases to be affiliated with the transferor, if it does, are given; affiliation could be decided from a world under section 251.1, but is given here. While the deemed owner is affiliated, the 30-day period in 40(3.4)(b)(i) does not begin; once affiliation ends, it is read as beginning that day. (c)(iii) (foreign affiliate liquidations) is not encoded.
56. **Dividends (82(1), 121).** Whether a dividend is an eligible dividend (89(14)) is a fact, as are the payer's residence and status and any dividend rental arrangement. Each gross-up and credit is rounded down to the cent. Where the consolidated text gives no rate for the year (non-eligible dividends before 2018, eligible dividends before 2006, the eligible credit before 2009), none is applied and the explanation says so; the historical rates are in the amending Acts. Dividends from non-resident corporations are left out (they are income from property under 90). The 260(5.1) deductions and paragraph (e) are not encoded. The deemed dividend under 84.1(1)(b) is taken as not eligible.
57. **Intercorporate dividends (112(1), 55(2)).** The payer's kind, whether the dividend is on a redemption to which 84(2) or (3) applies, safe income, whether the series includes each of the events in 55(3)(a)(i) to (v) involving an unrelated person (55(3.01)(a)), and whether 55(3)(b) applies are facts. The purpose (or result) tests in (2.1)(b) are judgment calls or facts; for an 84(3) dividend the "results" reading is folded into the same answer. After 55(5)(f) splits the dividend, (2.1)(c) is read as reaching the excess portion and not the safe-income portion, so only the excess is recharacterized. Part IV tax, 55(2.2) to (2.5), 55(3.01)(b) to (g), 112(2) to (2.9) and 138(6) are not encoded.
58. **Depreciable property (13, 20(16)).** Each class is computed at the end of a taxation year. C, D and D.1 are given as one total, as are E.1, E.2 and G to K. Capital cost allowance claimed (E) is given: its rates, the half-year rule and the available-for-use rules are in the Regulations and section 13(26) to (32), not encoded. Recapture and terminal loss are read at year end only; recapture is income from the business or property, not a capital amount. A disposition's capital gain uses capital cost as its adjusted cost base. 13(7) (change in use), 13(4) (replacement property) and 13(7.1) (assistance) are not encoded.
59. **Redemptions (84(3)).** The amount paid and the paid-up capital are for all the shares of the class redeemed at that time; the holder's share is by number of shares, rounded down to the cent. The holder's proceeds are its share of the amount paid less its dividend, plus whatever part of the dividend 55(2)(b) deems proceeds. For an individual the dividend is not eligible unless designated; for a corporation it goes to `IntercorporateDividends` with `onRedemption`. 84(2), 84(6) to (9) and 88(2)(b)(ii) are not encoded.
60. **Capital dividend account (89(1), 83(2)).** The account is kept in half-cents. The untaxed part of each gain is the gain less its taxable capital gain (so a gift taxed at nil under 38(a.1)(i) adds all of it); the period is taken to be the dispositions given. The (a)(i)(C) and (a)(ii)(C) amounts (accrual while held by a public, non-resident-controlled or exempt corporation) are given as one net amount, as are (b), (d) (proceeds less the policy's adjusted cost basis) and the other paragraphs. The election's timing and form are facts. Part III tax (184(2)) on an excessive election and the 184(3) election are not encoded.
61. **Share exchanges (86).** A reorganization of capital, the old shares being capital property and all of the taxpayer's shares of the class, and whether 85(1) applies are facts. Whether part of an excess of value benefits a related person is a fact or a judgment call (relied on only where there is an excess); the gift portion is all the excess unless given. The new shares' cost is shared by value and rounded down to the cent. An 84(3) dividend on the old shares where the boot exceeds their paid-up capital is not computed here (see `Redemption`). 86(2.1)(b) and 86(4) are not encoded.
62. **Replacement property (44(1)).** The kind of former property, whether what was acquired is a replacement property (44(5)), and the election are facts. Taxation years are numbered, and the deadlines are read as the end of the second (or first) taxation year after the initial year, which matches 24 (or 12) months for a calendar-year taxpayer. Only the initial year's gain is computed; the reserve in (e)(ii), (iii) and 44(1.1), 44(2) to (7), and 13(4) for depreciable property's recapture are not encoded.
63. **Identical properties (47(1)).** The pool keeps a count and a total cost rather than a per-property average, so the average is exact; a sale of some properties takes their share of the total, rounded down to the cent, and the rest stays in the pool (so the last sale picks up any remainder). Additions to cost, such as 53(1)(f), are added to the total. Which properties are identical is for the caller. A sale of more than the pool holds is read as a sale of all of it. 47(1)(c), (d), 47(2), (3) and pre-1972 properties are not encoded.
64. **Connected corporations (186(4)).** Holdings are given by holder (fully voting shares, and the value of all the holder's shares). Persons not at arm's length with the particular corporation are those related to it under section 251, plus any given as a question of fact. De jure control comes from the world's controls; "otherwise than by virtue of a right referred to in 251(5)(b)" is taken as reflected in the controls given. `SurplusStripping` and the qualified small business corporation share definition still take "connected" as a fact; this computes it for them.
65. **Returns of capital (84(4), (4.1), 40(3)).** The amount paid and paid-up capital reduced are for the whole class; each holder's share is by number of shares, rounded down. Whether a public corporation's distribution meets 84(4.1)(a) and (b) is a fact; if it does, the reduction is read as falling under 84(4). The holder's cost and 53(1) additions, and earlier 53(2) deductions, are given; 40(3) is applied at the time of the return, and 53(1)(a) adds the gain back so the adjusted cost base is nil. 93(1)(b), and partnership interests (40(3.1)), are not encoded.
66. **Winding-up (88(1)).** The conditions in the opening words are facts. Whether each property is eligible for the bump (capital property held since control was last acquired, and not ineligible under (c)(iii) to (vi), including the specified-shareholder rules in (c)(vi) and (c.2) to (c.9)) is a fact, as is each designation; designations are capped by (d)(ii) for each property and then by (d)(iii) in the order the properties are listed. The prescribed amount in (d)(ii) is nil. "Net tax cost" names (d)(i); its reserves are part of the liabilities given. Resource properties, partnership interests, section 80, (a.1) to (a.3), and (d.1) onward are not encoded.
67. **Amalgamations (87).** Whether a merger meets 87(1)(a) to (c) is a fact. Where 87(4) does not apply (consideration other than new shares, or no amalgamation), the old shares are read as disposed of at fair market value and the new shares as costing theirs. The gift portion is the whole excess of the old shares' value over the new where the benefit test is met (a fact or a judgment call). Only 87(2)(a) and (e) of the continuity rules are encoded; 87(1.1) to (3) and 87(4.1) onward are not.
68. **Convertible property (51).** Whether the exchange meets 51(1)(a) or (b) (including that the debt's terms confer the right) is a fact, as is whether 85 or 86 applies. Where 51(1) does not apply, the convertible property is read as disposed of at fair market value. 51(1)(d.1), (d.2), (e), (f) and 51(3) are not encoded.
69. **An individual's year.** Recapture is treated as business income and a terminal loss as a business loss of the year; dividends are included with the gross-up; 74.1 attributed income and losses are added. The capital gains deduction is computed after the net capital loss deduction, which enters its annual gains limit; Division C deductions are otherwise given. The dividend tax credit is reported, but tax under 117 and the credits in 118 are not computed. The individual's own 74.2-attributed amounts enter through the capital gains year.
70. **The Canadian securities election (39(4)–(6)).** The election year is given; it applies to that year and every later one. Whether the taxpayer is a trader or dealer is a fact or a judgment call; the other 39(5) statuses and whether a security is prescribed are facts. Where the election applies, the disposition's capital account becomes a fact, replacing any judgment call.
71. **Bad debts and worthless shares (50(1)).** Whether a debt was established to have become bad in the year and whether dissolution is reasonably expected ((b)(iii)(D)) are facts or judgment calls; (b)(iii)(A) to (C) are one fact. Whether the debt or share gives a business investment loss (a small business corporation's, 39(1)(c)) is a fact. The deemed disposition is dated at the taxation year end, and carries `deemedBy := some .s50` for the superficial loss definition.
72. **Part of a property (43(1)).** The portion is given as a measure of the part over the whole (by default, fair market values); whether that measure is reasonable is a fact or a judgment call. It is rounded down to the cent, and the rest of the cost stays with the part kept.
73. **Corporate attribution (74.4(2)).** The main-purpose test and whether the corporation is a small business corporation are facts or judgment calls; whether a designated person is a specified shareholder (as 74.4(2)(a) reads that definition) is a fact. The prescribed rate is given in basis points a year and the period in months; the deemed interest is rounded down. (f) uses the 82(1) inclusion (with the gross-up) of the dividends on the shares taken back. 74.4(3) to (11) and 74.5(1) as it applies here are not encoded.
74. **Qualified farm or fishing property (110.6(2)).** The kind of property, family ownership, use in a Canadian farming or fishing business, and the 110.6(1.3) conditions are facts, except that the gross revenue and "actively engaged on a regular and continuous basis" test may be a judgment call; family farm or fishing corporations and partnerships are facts. (2.1)(a) is read as the one lifetime limit less the year's 110.6(2) deduction (the prescribed form's reading), so the two deductions together never exceed it (proven); read literally, (2.1)(a) repeats the (2)(a) formula, which counts only preceding years' deductions. 110.6(1.3)(c) (property acquired before June 18, 1987) is not encoded. With farm property in the year, a larger cumulative net investment loss can shrink the (2) deduction and so leave more room for (2.1).
75. **Split income (120.4).** Ages are the age attained before the year. Each amount is excluded or not as a whole; an amount partly excluded is given as two amounts. Related business, excluded business and reasonable return are facts or judgment calls (relied on only for individuals 17 or older before the year, for whom they matter); inheritance, 160(4) transfers, exempt gains, safe harbour capital returns and excluded shares are facts. Split income covers private dividends (with the gross-up), section 15 benefits and related-business partnership and trust income, given as amounts; 120.4(4) and (5) gains are taken as given. The highest rate is 33% (117(2)(e)); the 120.4(3) B and C amounts are given, and D is the dividend tax credit on the dividends in split income. Foreign tax credits (126) are not encoded.
76. **The federal rates (117(2)).** The bracket amounts default to those in the text (2025); for later years the amounts indexed under 117.1 are given. The rates are those of the consolidated text (14% at the bottom); a transitional rate for 2025 in the amending Act's application provisions is not applied. Tax is computed on the whole amount and rounded down to the cent. In the individual's year, taxable income (in half-cents) is halved and rounded down; federal tax is that plus tax on split income, less the dividend tax credit and other credits given (section 118 is not encoded), and never below nil; 117(2.1) and 120.4(3) B and C are not applied there.
77. **Personal amounts (118(1), (1.1)).** The indexed amounts for the year are given, defaulting to those in the text ($12,298, $15,000, $2,150); F is read as the amount for 2023 and later. The phase-out thresholds are the 117(2)(d) and (e) amounts for the year. The enhancement allowed is rounded down to the cent, as is the credit at 14% (the lowest 117(2) rate). Only (a) and (c) of 118(1)'s B are encoded; the individual's income for the year is taken from section 3.
78. **Donations (118.1).** Total charitable gifts (including the five preceding years' gifts not yet deducted, and the eligible amount of each) are given. B, C and D of the "total gifts" formula are given as one amount; E is nil. The credit is rounded down to the cent, and the excess of total charitable gifts over total gifts is reported as carried forward. Gifts of listed securities enter the capital gains computation through 38(a.1) (nil inclusion), so B is nil for them.
79. **Corporate rates (123 to 125).** The rates are those for days after 2018 (the 13% and 19% percentages); a year straddling an earlier change is not prorated. The business limit is given after association (125(2), (3)) and the 125(5.1) grinds. Active business income, aggregate investment income (129(4)) and personal services business income are given. 125(1)(b)(i) to (iii) are nil. The refundable tax on investment income (123.3), Part IV tax and the dividend refund are not encoded.
80. **Refundable taxes (123.3, 129, 186).** The opening balances of the two accounts (last year's balance less last year's refunds from it) are given. Foreign tax credits (126) are nil in the 129(4) formulas. The Part IV reduction for losses claimed (186(1)(c), (d)) is 38⅓% of the losses, given (see note 110). 186(1)(b) amounts (a connected payer's refund attributable to the dividend) are given, split by the payer's account. The 129(4) cap at Part I tax uses the 123 to 125 tax plus 123.3. Eligible dividends draw only on the eligible account; non-eligible dividends draw on the non-eligible account and then what is left of the eligible one. 38⅓%, 10⅔% and 30⅔% are applied exactly and rounded down to the cent.
81. **Shareholder loans (15(2), 80.4).** Who the borrower and lender are, and whether a loan is pertinent, are facts; whether a repayment was part of a series is a fact or a judgment call. "Within one year after the end of the taxation year" is read as on or before the same date a year later. The prescribed rate is given in basis points for a whole year and the period in months; interest paid within 30 days after the year is given. 15(2.2) to (2.4) (including the employee exceptions), 15(2.17) and the 20(1)(j) exclusion for amounts already deducted in computing taxable income are not encoded.
82. **The general rate income pool (89(1), 185.1(1)).** The pool at the end of the preceding year is given, and may be negative. The general rate factor is taken as 0.72 throughout (days after 2011). E is eligible dividends received (paragraph (a)); foreign affiliate dividends (b), F (89(4) to (6)) and B (specified future tax consequences) are nil. Adjusted taxable income uses the 125(1) amount as the income on which the small business deduction was claimed (100/19 of the deduction). The excessive designation is computed for the year's eligible dividends together, not dividend by dividend; whether one of the main purposes was to artificially maintain or increase the pool is a fact or a judgment call. A 185.1(2) election is taken to be made on time (the 90-day limit, and 185.1(3)(b)'s 30 months or every shareholder's concurrence, are not checked); concurrence under 185.1(3)(a) is a fact. The election is applied to the year's eligible dividends together. 185.1(4) is not encoded. Amounts are rounded down to the cent.
83. **The minimum tax (127.5 to 127.531).** Only the 127.52(1) assumptions in (d), (d.1), (f), (h)(ii) and (i)(ii) are encoded; the others are taken not to apply. Subdivision E deductions and other Division C deductions are deducted in full ((j), which halves carrying charges and some others, is not encoded). The net capital loss deduction is the one computed for regular tax, which (i)(ii)(B)(IV) reaches for losses of years beginning after 2023; for losses of earlier years (III) would allow more. Allowable business investment losses stay at ½, since (d) does not reach 38(c); the full capital loss is then an allowable capital loss under 3(b), less the ABIL. Amounts attributed to the individual under 74.2 are doubled. The basic minimum tax credit takes ½ of the personal credit and of the other credits given (as if all were 118-series credits) and 4/5 of the donation credit; the dividend tax credit is not part of it. "Tax payable under Division E" is the federal tax computed before the minimum tax, with tax on split income; the minimum tax is not reduced for split income (120.4(3)), The 120.2 carryforward is given by year and amount (what is left after earlier deductions) and claimed in full; section 126 is not encoded, so the foreign tax adjustments are nil, and 120.2(4) is not encoded.
84. **Interest on borrowed money (20(1)(c), 20(3)).** Each borrowing is a fact: its principal, the interest for the year (paid or payable, as the individual's method requires), whether there is a legal obligation, its use, and for a refinancing the use of the borrowing it repaid (already traced through any earlier refinancing). The use is the current, direct use of the money (Bronfman Trust, Singleton); whether it was for the purpose of earning income is a fact or a judgment call (Ludco). Where part of the principal is traced to the use, the interest is apportioned by principal. "A reasonable amount in respect thereof" is given where the rate is not reasonable. In an individual's year the deduction is taken as a loss from a source under 3(d) rather than netted against the property's income, which gives the same income where the year's income is not nil. For the minimum tax, 127.52(1)(j)(ii) halves the part on money borrowed to earn income from property; interest on business borrowing is deducted in full.
85. **The medical expense credit (118.2(1)).** The threshold defaults to $2,834, the 2025 indexed amount of $1,813, and is a field for other years. Whether an amount is a medical expense under 118.2(2) and (2.1) to (4) is a fact or a judgment call for each expense; so are the receipts, earlier or other claims, and whether it was paid in the chosen 12-month (or 24-month) period. A dependant is identified by name and income; a child under 18 at the end of the year is in B with the family. The appropriate percentage is 14%, as elsewhere. The individual's income is the section 3 income of the year; for the minimum tax, 127.531(b) takes half the credit.
86. **The disability tax credit (118.3(1)).** Paragraphs (a) and (a.1) are one question, a fact or a judgment call; (a.2) and (a.3), the practitioner's certificate in prescribed form by the practitioner the impairment calls for, are one fact. The amounts default to the 2025 indexed values ($10,138, $5,914 and $3,464). Paragraph (c) is a fact, not derived from the year's medical expenses. The care and supervision expenses in C are those included in a deduction under 63, 64 or 118.2 for any year, given. The credit is at 14%; for the minimum tax, 127.531(a) takes half of it. For 118.3(2), the supporter's (a) conditions (claimed or could have claimed the 118(1)(b) or (d) amount for the person, with the listed relationship), the person's residence and whether their spouse claims a 118 or 118.8 amount are facts; (b) is read from the person's own 118.3(1)(c) fact. The person's tax under (d) is tax at the 117(2) rates, plus tax on split income, less the 118(1) personal credit; 118.01 to 118.07 and 118.7 are not encoded. The amounts are credits, not the underlying amounts; 118.3(3), sharing between supporters, is not encoded.
87. **Pension income and splitting (118(2), (3), (7), (8), 60.03).** Each payment is a fact: a pension plan life annuity (qualified pension income), a registered fund payment (RRSP annuity, RRIF and the like: pension income, qualified only if received on a spouse's death), or a government benefit (OAS, CPP: neither). The 118(8)(c) to (f) exclusions are taken not to apply. The 118(2) amounts default to the 2025 indexed values ($9,028 and $45,522); income for 118(2) is the section 3 income, without the section 79 and 20(1)(ww) adjustments. The split-pension amount elected is capped at the 60.03(1) limit; the months married are 12 unless given. Under 60.03(2)(a) the pensioner's eligible pension income is reduced by the amount split; under (b) the transferee's is increased by it if the transferee is 65 or over, or if it was qualified pension income to the pensioner (a fact). The joint election's validity (filing, 60.03(3), (4)) is assumed, and both spouses' years must be computed with matching amounts. The credits are at 14%; for the minimum tax, 127.531(a) takes half.
88. **The OAS recovery tax (180.2, 60(w)).** Old Age Security is a payment of its own kind: included in income, not pension income. Adjusted income is the section 3 income without the 60(w) deduction; the other 180.2(1) adjustments are taken not to apply. The threshold defaults to the 2025 indexed $93,454. B, the Part XIII rate, is nil (a resident), and 60(n)(i) repayments are nil. The tax is deducted under 60(w) in 3(c), so income for the age credit, the other credits and the minimum tax is after it. Federal tax (`IndividualYear.federalTax`) is Part I tax only; the Part I.2 tax is `IndividualYear.oasRecovery`. While encoding this, the minimum tax's income was found to leave out pension income and the split-pension amounts; it now includes them, with the 60(c) and 60(w) deductions.
89. **Open question, not encoded: 118.8 (transfer of unused credits to a spouse).** As consolidated, C(a) is the spouse's tax payable if no Division E amount were deductible other than under 118(1)(c), 118(10) and sections 118.01 to 118.07, 118.3, 118.61 and 118.7. 90. **The eligible dependant amount (118(1)(b)).** Each condition in (i), (ii) and (A) to (D) is a fact; the age and infirmity tests in (D) are one fact, and D's infirmity test (the 18-or-over and the under-18 branches) is one fact. 118(4)(a) and (b) (one person per individual, one individual per person or home, and no claim when claimants disagree) and (a.1) are left to the facts: only one dependant is given, and the claim is made only if no 118(1)(a) claim is. 91. **An increase in paid-up capital (84(1), 53(1)(b)).** The paid-up capital increase, the net asset increase or net liability decrease from the transaction, the reduction of other classes, and the part of the increase from a conversion of contributed surplus that qualifies under (c.1) to (c.3) are facts. Reading the opening words with (d) to (f), a transaction within (b) or (c) for less than the increase still leaves a dividend of the difference. For 53(1)(b), the part a corporate holder deducted under 112(1) that would be subject to 55(2) as a separate dividend is given.
92. **Employment, CPP and EI (118(10), 118.7, 60(e.1)).** Employment income, EI premiums and the base and enhanced CPP contributions are facts (computed under the Employment Insurance Act and the Canada Pension Plan); each is capped at its 2025 maximum by default. 99. **Moving expenses (62(1)).** The moving expenses paid (62(3)) and not paid by an employer on the taxpayer's behalf are given; so is the income for the year from the new work location (or, for a student, the 56(1)(n) and (o) amounts). 62(1)(b), with (c), is read as letting expenses not deducted in a year because of (c) be deducted in a later year: they are given as carried. The distances are given in whole kilometres (the shortest normal route, Giannakopoulos v. M.N.R.); "at least 40 km farther" is the old distance being at least the new distance plus 40. Whether the move was to enable the work or study, and whether the taxpayer ordinarily resided at each residence, are facts. 62(2) (moves from or to outside Canada) is not encoded. While encoding this, the Subdivision E deductions computed in an individual's year were gathered into one definition used by both the year and the minimum tax.
100. **The standby charge (6(1)(e), 6(2)).** The available days, the days owned, the cost, the lease payments and the insurance in them, the personal kilometres, and what the employee paid for the use of the car (not operating expenses) are facts; whether the employee was required to use the car for work and drove it primarily for work are facts. Days over 30 are rounded only when the quotient exceeds 1 and is not whole (so 30 days or fewer is a fraction of a month), to the nearest whole number with halves rounded down, separately for B (all available days) and D (days owned). The charge is computed as an exact fraction and rounded down once. In an individual's year the benefit is employment income, and counts as earned income for section 63. 6(1)(k) (operating costs, at the prescribed rate) and 6(2.1) (salespersons, 1.5%) are not encoded.
101. **The stop-loss on shares (112(3), (3.01), (6.1)).** The dividends received on the share are given, each with its kind, whether it was received under 84(3), whether it passed between private corporations, and whether the 5% test in (3.01)(a) was met when it was received; whether the share was owned throughout the 365 days before the disposition is one fact. "Received on the share" is read as received on the share disposed of (dividends on identical shares pooled under 47 are not apportioned). For a corporation, the part of a taxable dividend deducted under 112 is given (the deduction under 115(1) or 138(6) likewise). The reduction is applied as a lower adjusted cost base where there is a loss, which gives the same loss and leaves gains alone. The holder is an individual or a corporation; trusts and partnerships (112(3.1) to (3.32)) and shares that are not capital property (112(4) to (4.22)) are not encoded.
102. **A distribution on winding-up (84(2)).** The amount or value distributed to the class, and the paid-up capital reduction on it, are facts; so are the holder's and the outstanding shares immediately before. Whether the distribution was "on the winding-up, discontinuance or reorganization" of the business is a fact or a judgment call (it is read broadly in the case law, "in any manner whatever"). The holder's proceeds are its share of what was distributed less its dividend, under 54 "proceeds of disposition" (j), except to the extent 55(2) applies (not applied here). 88(2) (a winding-up after 1978 into a parent) and the interaction with 84.1 are not encoded.
103. **Shareholder benefits (15(1)).** The value of the benefit, what the shareholder paid for it, and the part section 84 deems a dividend are facts (valuing a benefit, such as personal use of corporate property, is often itself contested; the value given is taken). Whether it was conferred in the person's capacity as shareholder, not as an employee or otherwise, is a fact or a judgment call. The way it was conferred decides (a) to (d); 88(1), (2) is excepted only for a resident corporation and 86.1(1) only for a non-resident one, the other ways for both. Benefits to persons other than the shareholder (15(1.4), 56(2), 246(1)) and the corporation's side are not encoded. While encoding this, the newer income sources in an individual's year were gathered into one definition used by both the year and the minimum tax.
104. **The small business investment rollover (44.1).** Whether the shares disposed of and the replacement shares are eligible small business corporation shares (a common share issued to the individual, the issuer an eligible small business corporation, the group's assets carried at $100 million or less before and after) is a fact for each; whether the corporation was an active business corporation throughout the individual's ownership is a fact or a judgment call. "Owned throughout the 185-day period that ended immediately before the disposition" is read as acquired at least 185 days before the disposition date. A replacement share is acquired in the calendar year of the disposition or the next, and designated. The capital gain before 44.1 is given. 44.1(3) to (12) and 44.1(2)(c) are not encoded.
105. **Personal-use property (46).** Whether a property is personal-use property (54) is taken from the disposition being given here (this module is applied to it), and whether it is excluded property given in the circumstances of 110.1(1) or 118.1(1) is a fact. For a part (46(2)) the whole property's adjusted cost base is given; for a set (46(3)), it is the set's total, and whether the properties would ordinarily be disposed of as a set is a fact or a judgment call. The floor is applied before 40(2)(g)(iii), which still makes a loss on personal-use property (other than listed personal property) nil. 46(4) is not encoded.
106. **Employee stock options (7, 110(1)(d), (d.1)).** The value of the securities when acquired, what was paid for them and for the option, and the dates are facts. 7(1.1) applies where the share is of a CCPC and the employee was at arm's length with the corporations named (one fact). The 110(1)(d) conditions are reduced to four facts (a prescribed share; not a non-qualified security, which covers the 110(1.31) vesting limit and the employer's designation; the amount payable at least the value when the agreement was made; the employee at arm's length); (d.1) needs the share kept two years (730 days) after acquisition, not disposed of other than on death. The deduction is in computing taxable income, not income. For the minimum tax, 127.52(1)(h) as consolidated does not list 110(1)(d) or (d.1), so neither is deducted. 7(1)(b) to (e), 7(1.4), 110(1)(d.01) and 110(1.1) are not encoded.
107. **A corporation's charitable gifts (110.1(1)(a)).** Each gift's eligible amount less what was deducted for it before is given, with its year; a gift is available in the year it is made and the five taxation years after, read as calendar years. B (the taxable capital gain on each gift, in proportion to the eligible amount over the proceeds), C (40(1.01) gains) and D (recapture on gifted depreciable property, up to the gain over capital cost) are given. A is the corporation's income (137(2), for credit unions, is not encoded). The deduction is taken in full up to the limit; the order in which gifts are used (oldest first in practice) does not change the total. 110.1(1)(b) to (d), (1.1), (2.1), (3) and (6) are not encoded, nor is the corporation's taxable income computation it feeds.
108. **A corporation's year (2(2), 3, 129(4)).** Active business income (net of active business losses, for 125(1)(a)), personal services business income, and income from property are given, already classified: what is incident to an active business, and what is a specified investment business, is the caller's call (129(4) "income or loss", 125(7)). Taxable dividends received are income (12(1)(j), (k)), except the part 55(2) deems not a dividend: that part is a capital gain of its own (55(2)(c)), or, on a redemption, added to the share's proceeds (55(2)(b)), which the caller puts in the disposition. Corporations take no gross-up. Taxable income deducts gifts (110.1, its limit on the year's income), the 112(1) deduction, net capital losses (111(1)(b)) and then non-capital losses of other years (111(1)(a), note 109); none takes it below zero. For "aggregate investment income", every taxable capital gain and allowable capital loss is taken as an eligible portion (no gain accrued outside a CCPC), allowable business investment losses count among allowable capital losses, and a dividend is investment income only to the extent it is not deducted (so a foreign dividend is, 113 aside). Part IV applies to assessable dividends: those deductible under 112(1), less any 55(2) part; 186(1)(c) and (d) are not encoded. Taxable income and investment income are handed to the rate modules in cents, rounded down. The pool's eligible dividends received are those designated eligible, and the capital dividend account adds the year's dispositions (including 55(2) gains) to those of the earlier period, given.
109. **Non-capital losses (111(8), 111(1)(a), 111(3)).** For a corporation, E is its losses from business and property, the 112(1) deduction, the 111(1)(b) deduction and its allowable business investment loss; 110.1 gifts are not in E (they carry forward on their own), nor is the 111(1)(a) deduction itself. F is 3(c). B (110.5, 115(1)(a)(vii)) is not encoded; D (the farm loss), D.1 (111(10)) and D.2 (section 80) are given together. "Before the taxpayer's eleventh following taxation year" is read with years as calendar years: the ABIL counts through the tenth year after, which is when NetCapitalLoss's `ExpiredBIL` turns its unused part into a net capital loss. Each carried loss is given with what is left of it (111(3)(a)); 186(1)(c) and (d) claims are not encoded. A claim is applied oldest first (111(3)(b)), carrybacks included, with a later year's loss counted after every earlier one. In a corporation's year the default claim is what brings taxable income to nil and no more, so nothing is wasted; any other amount can be claimed. Losses of years ending before 2006 (7 or 10 years) and the acquisition of control rules (111(4) to (5.5)) are not encoded.
110. **Losses claimed against Part IV tax (186(1)(c), (d)).** Part IV tax is the 186(1)(a) and (b) amounts less 38⅓% of the non-capital losses claimed, never below nil. (c) is limited to the year's own non-capital loss; (d) to what is left of other years' available losses after the year's 111(1)(a) deduction, taken oldest first ("not exceeding the portion that would have been deductible under section 111 ... if the corporation had sufficient income"). What is claimed comes off the losses carried forward (111(3)(a)(ii)). The Act does not say which account the reduced Part IV tax leaves: the reduction is taken off the Part IV tax on non-eligible dividends first, so the eligible refundable dividend tax on hand keeps the eligible part as far as possible. This is the reading most favourable to the corporation; **please confirm it, or tell me the allocation you use** (pro rata is the other natural reading). Farm losses are not encoded.
111. **The business limit (125(2), (3), (5)(b), (5.1)).** Whether the corporation is associated with another CCPC (256) is a fact; so is the agreement's percentage for it and the total assigned, in basis points. Without an agreement the limit is nil (the Minister's allocation under 125(4) is not encoded). "Less than 51 weeks" is read as fewer than 357 days. C, taxable capital employed in Canada (181.2 to 181.4), is given, already chosen by the case in 125(5.1)(a)(i) to (iii); E, the adjusted aggregate investment income of the corporation and every corporation associated with it at any time in the year, for their years ending in the preceding calendar year, is given, and `CorporateYear.adjustedAggregateInvestmentIncome` computes each corporation's part. Each grind is applied to the limit after sharing and proration (A and D are the limit "but for this subsection"), with one rounding down. For adjusted aggregate investment income, "active asset" (125(7)) is the caller's classification, by naming the dispositions; life insurance policy amounts and the 91(4) exclusion are not encoded; 55(2) gains count unless the share was an active asset. 125(5)(a) is not encoded.
112. **Associated corporations (256(1)).** Control is read from the world's controls, de jure or de facto (256(5.1)), each a fact or a judgment call; the 256(1.2)(c) value test is applied by the caller in stating them. "Related" is section 251 as computed on the same world, and a person is related to himself (256(1.5), read without its condition that he own shares of both). In (d), the person may be a member of the group that controls the other corporation. In (e), "one or more persons who were members of both related groups, either alone or together" is read as all the members common to both groups, together. **Please check (b) with 256(1.2)(b)(i):** two controllers are the same group if they have the same members, and also if every member of either owns shares of both corporations, since a corporation controlled by one member of a group of its shareholders is "considered to be controlled by that group". Read literally this associates two corporations owned 51/49 and 49/51 by unrelated persons, which is the result I understand CRA to take; if you read (b)(i) more narrowly (for example, requiring the group to act together, as in *Southside Car Market*), the test in `paraB` should drop its second limb. Shareholdings are given by class, with the specified class (256(1.1)) a fact. **256(2)** is applied over the corporations listed: a chain of third corporations of any length is followed ("or is deemed by this subsection to be associated with"). For section 125 every third corporation on the chain must be a CCPC that has not elected under 256(2)(b)(ii); where two corporations are linked through more than one third, one that qualifies is enough. An electing third's business limit is nil (`BusinessLimit.Facts.electedThird`). For the 25% tests (only), shares owned by a corporation are deemed owned by its shareholders in proportion to the value of their shares (256(1.2)(d)), through any number of holding corporations, each level's value percentages given; the result is kept in basis points and rounded down at each level. Control is not recomputed from the look-through: the caller states control with it in mind. 256(1.3): a parent is deemed to own a minor child's shares where the other corporation is controlled by the parent or a group that includes the parent; whether the child manages the business without significant influence from the parent is a judgment call, and whether the child is under 18 at the time is given. The look-through extends to partnerships (each member's share of income or loss, 256(1.2)(e); the $1,000,000 rule for a nil year is the caller's) and trusts (100% to each discretionary beneficiary, otherwise by the value of the beneficial interest, and 100% to the 75(2) contributor, 256(1.2)(f)), the percentages given. 256(1.4)(a): a right to acquire shares (or control their votes), other than one exercisable only on a death, bankruptcy or permanent disability, makes the holder their owner; where the right is to newly issued shares, they are deemed issued, enlarging the class for every holder's percentage; a right over existing shares does not enlarge it (its owner keeps them too). 256(1.4)(b): a right to have another shareholder's shares redeemed, acquired or cancelled (not one exercisable only on a death, bankruptcy or disability) takes those shares out of the class, for every holder's percentage; its effect on control is the caller's. 256(2.1) is one judgment call for the pair, whether one of the main reasons for their separate existence is to reduce tax.
113. **Meals and entertainment (67.1).** The reasonable amount, and which 67.1(2) exception applies, are given (an exception may be a judgment call); the conditions of (e) and (e.1) (the remote site's distance from a population centre, the construction camp) are taken together as the fact that the exception applies. **Please check (f):** "one of six or fewer special events held in a calendar year" is read so that if seven or more are held, none of them is excepted; CRA's administrative position may instead except the first six. The truck drivers' specified percentage is by calendar year, with all of 2007 at 60% (the Act starts it March 19, 2007). For 67.1(3) the $50 a day is the Act's amount (no other has been prescribed), capped at the fee. 67.1 applies "for the purposes of this Act" other than 62, 63, 118.01 and 118.2; the module gives the deemed amounts and leaves where they are deducted to the caller.
114. **Financing expenses (20(1)(e), (e.1)).** The purpose ((i) to (ii.2)), whether the expense is an excluded amount (iv.1), whether the money or property is for exempt income, and the settlement of (v) (all the debt obligations settled or extinguished, not as part of a series of refinancings, for consideration that is not the taxpayer's or a non-arm's length person's securities) are facts. (v) is applied only to borrowings and indebtedness ((ii) and (ii.1)), as its words say; a restructuring's costs continue at 20%. The 20% is prorated by the days in the year over 365 in every year, so a 366-day year allows slightly more, still capped by what is left. A fee under (e.1) that relates to more than one year is not deducted under (e.1); the module does not then move it to (e). 20(1)(e)(vi) is not encoded.
115. **Work space in the home (18(12), 8(13)).** The expenses are the work space's share (the apportionment is the caller's); (a)(i) and (ii) are facts or judgment calls. For a business, the income limit is the business income computed without the work space amount and without 34.1 (the caller supplies it); for employment, the employment income without the work space deduction. **Please check (c):** an amount carried in is deemed otherwise deductible "subject to (a) and (b)"; if the space does not qualify under (a) in the year, the amount is not deducted and, read literally, does not carry further (it is not then undeductible "by reason only of" (b)). If you take the view that it survives to a later qualifying year, `carriedForward` should keep the carried-in amount when (a) fails.
116. **Doubtful and bad debts (20(1)(l)(i), (p)(i), 12(1)(d), (i)).** The reasonable reserve for each doubtful debt is given (its reasonableness is the caller's, often a judgment); it is capped at the debt. A debt established to be bad in the year is written off in full, not reserved for. Only debts included in income for the year or an earlier year qualify (trade receivables, say); a loan or other debt never included in income is a capital matter (50(1), 39(1)(c)). Recoveries are given in total. The parts for lenders and financial institutions (20(1)(l)(ii), (p)(ii), 12(1)(i.1)) are not encoded, nor is the partial write-off of a debt.
117. **Amounts received in advance (12(1)(a), 20(1)(m), 12(1)(e)(i)).** The reasonable reserve for each advance (what is reasonably anticipated to be delivered, rendered, rented or repaid after the year) is given, and capped at the advance; bottle deposits take none (20(1)(m)(iv)). An advance received in an earlier year and still undelivered keeps its reserve, the previous reserve coming back in under 12(1)(e). 20(6) (tickets and similar), 20(1)(m.1) and 20(7) are not encoded; 20(1)(n) with 20(8) is note 118.
118. **The reserve for unpaid amounts on property sold (20(1)(n), 20(8)).** The "reasonable amount" is taken as the profit times the part unpaid at the year end over the price (the usual measure), unless another amount is given; it is capped at the profit. "Not due until at least two years after" the sale is tested on the latest due date agreed at the sale; "more than 36 months before the end of the year" compares the sale date plus three years with the year end. 20(8)(a), (c) and (d) are facts.
119. **Prepaid expenses (18(9)).** "Can reasonably be regarded as" relating to a period is read as day by day over the period the outlay covers, rounded down, with each year deducting the share of its days; in the year paid, everything up to the year end is deducted (including any past days, which 18(9) does not touch). Prepaid interest of a corporation, partnership or trust is not carried by (b); whether 20(1)(c) allows it in a later year ("paid or payable in respect of the year") is not encoded. The exceptions in (a)(ii) and (iii) for insurers and group term life insurance, (iv) and (c) to (e) are not encoded; neither is the 28(1) cash method.
120. **Thin capitalization (18(4)).** For a corporation resident in Canada. The months are the calendar months ending in the year; for each, the greatest outstanding debts to specified non-residents in the month, and, at its beginning, the contributed surplus contributed by specified non-resident shareholders and the paid-up capital of the classes they own, are given (who is a specified non-resident shareholder, and which debts count, is the caller's). Averages and 1.5 times equity are rounded down to the cent. Not encoded: trusts and non-resident corporations (18(5)(b), (c)), 18(4.1) to (8) (back-to-back loans and the like), 18.2, and 214(16)(b). The interest denied is deemed a dividend in full for Part XIII (214(16)(a)), all of the interest for the year being paid or deemed paid by its end (214(17)(a)); the Part XIII rate is 25% unless a treaty rate is given. Withholding on the interest that remains interest (212(1)(b)) is not encoded.
121. **Part XIII on dividends and interest (212(1)(b), (2)).** Arm's length, "fully exempt interest" and "participating debt interest" are facts; the treaty rate is given (as is whether the payee is entitled to it), and a rate above 25% does not raise the tax. For 212(4), whether a service was performed in the ordinary course of the non-resident's business of performing it for a fee, and whether a payment reimburses a specific expense, are facts; the reasonable part is given (by default all of it), and only the excess is a management fee. Treaties often exempt management fees as business profits; the rate is then given as 0. Withholding and remittance (215), the 227(8) penalty, and the other payments of 212(1) (rents and royalties, pensions and the rest) are not encoded.
122. **A non-resident's rent (212(1)(d), 216(1), 120(1), 118.94).** For an individual only. The net rental income (rent less expenses and capital cost allowance, computed as though resident) is given; all of it is taken as not earned in a province, so the 120(1) addition is 48% of the tax. Part I tax uses the year's indexed brackets. Under 118.94 the personal credits are allowed only if all or substantially all the individual's income is taxable income earned in Canada (a fact); the amount of those credits is given. Credits 118.94 leaves (such as donations) are not encoded, nor are 216(4) and (5), the election's time limits, or a corporation's election with branch tax (219).
123. **Buying from a non-resident (116(5), (5.01), (5.3)).** Whether the property is taxable Canadian property is computed from 248(1) when the property is given (note 124); whether it is excluded (116(6)) or 116(5.2) property is a fact; so are the treaty conditions and notice of 116(5.01). "After reasonable inquiry the purchaser had no reason to believe that the non-resident person was not resident in Canada" is a fact or judgment call. The certificate limit (116(2)) or amount fixed (116(5.2)) is given; a 116(4) certificate excuses capital property only, as 116(5.3) has no counterpart to 116(5)(b). The cost to the purchaser is used for 116(5) and the amount payable for 116(5.3). 116(5.4), interest and penalties are not encoded.
124. **Taxable Canadian property (248(1)).** For (d) and (e), each month of the 60-month period is given with the percentage of value derived from Canadian real, resource and timber property and options or interests in them; the caller excludes what is derived through corporations, partnerships or trusts whose shares or interests were not themselves taxable Canadian property at the time. "At any particular time during the 60-month period" is read month by month (a month counts if the test is met at some time in it); in (e) the 25% holding and the value test must be met at the same particular time. More than 50% is strict. (g) to (k) (which apply only for 2, 107(2.001), 128.1, 150 and certain 85 and 97 purposes) are not encoded; neither are the deeming rules elsewhere in the Act that make property taxable Canadian property (such as 44.1(2) or 85(1)(i)).
125. **Becoming and ceasing to be resident (128.1(1), (4)).** On arrival (128.1(1)), the deemed disposition happens while non-resident, so only its new cost is computed; whether the property is taxable Canadian property is decided by 248(1) where the property is given; Canadian business inventory and Class 14.1 property, and excluded rights (other than 128.1(10)(k)), are facts. On departure, for an individual. Each property's class under (b)(i) to (v) is a fact: Canadian real, resource and timber resource property; property of a business carried on through a permanent establishment in Canada; an excluded right or interest (128.1(10)); property owned on last becoming resident or inherited since, which is excepted only for an individual resident no more than 60 of the 120 months ending at emigration; and property elected under 128.1(6)(a). Each deemed disposition is a disposition on capital account at fair market value, so it enters 3(b) with the rest of the year's dispositions (and 40(2)(g) and the other loss rules apply as they would). The 220(4.5) security election, the 128.1(4)(d) election, (b.1), (6) to (8), and the rules for corporations and trusts are not encoded; neither is the 128.1(10) reporting.
126. **The student loan interest credit (118.62).** The appropriate percentage is the lowest 117(2) rate, 14% as encoded (the 2025 rate of 14.5% is not applied, as note 93 says). Which loans qualify (federal acts, the Apprentice Loans Act, or a provincial student-assistance law) and whether an amount was paid under a judgment are facts; interest paid by a related person counts. In an individual's year, the claim is limited to the interest whose credit the tax left after the tuition credits can absorb, so the rest carries forward rather than being wasted; the credit is applied after tuition and before the dividend, donation, medical and other credits.
127. **Reversionary trusts (75(2), (3)).** Whether the trust holds the property on a 75(2)(a) or (b) condition is a fact or a judgment call (often one, reading the deed and the trustees' powers); so are the 75(3) exclusions. "The person from whom the property ... was directly or indirectly received" is the caller's, including the *Sommerer* reading that a sale at fair market value is not such a receipt. The amounts given are those for the year from the property and substituted property. Not encoded: 107(4.1), the effect on the contributor's own cost, and the interaction with 74.1 to 74.5 where both could apply.
128. **Distributions of trust property (107(2), (2.1), (4.1)).** The distribution is taken to be in satisfaction of a capital interest. Whether 75(2) applied at some time to the trust's property (read without "while the person is resident in Canada", and with the old 75(3)(c.2) exception gone), and the property was not since disposed of at fair market value, is a fact; so is whether the beneficiary is the contributor or the contributor's spouse or common-law partner (73(1)), and whether the contributor exists. 94(8.2) and 107.4(3) chains are not encoded. The cost amounts, the capital interest's adjusted cost base and cost amount, and eligible offsets are given. 107(2)(d) to (f) for depreciable property, (2.001), (2.002), (2.2), (4) and (5) are not encoded.
129. **The 21-year rule (104(4)).** The trust's kind is given: a trust under (a) (spousal or common-law partner, alter ego, joint) with the day of the death that matters, or another trust with its creation day. The days are the first (the death, or 21 years after the later of January 1, 1972 and creation), then every 21 years ((b)(iii) and (c)); a February 29 anniversary in a non-leap year is kept as given. (a.1) to (a.4), the transitional parts of (b), the alter ego trust's election out (ii.1), 104(5) for depreciable property and 104(5.8) are not encoded; exempt property (108(1)) and whether property is capital property are facts.
130. **A trust's income paid to beneficiaries (104(6)(b), (13), (19), (21), (24)).** The amount that became payable to each beneficiary (paid in the year, or enforceable in the year, 104(24)) and the trust's designations are given. A designation is limited to the beneficiary's amount payable ("part of the amount that ... was included"), dividends first and then gains, and the designations together to the trust's totals, in the order the beneficiaries are listed. Gains cannot be designated for a non-resident beneficiary (unless the trust is a mutual fund trust, not encoded); nothing can be designated by a trust not resident throughout the year. The beneficiary grosses up designated dividends under 82(1) (or deducts them under 112(1)); the trust's own gross-up of retained dividends, 104(6)(b)(i) to (iv), (7) to (7.1), (12), (14) and 105 are not encoded. The trust's tax (122(1)(a)) is taken on what it keeps, as its taxable income (Division C deductions aside), at 33%, or for a graduated rate estate or qualified disability trust (a fact) at the year's 117(2) rates; the dividends the trust keeps (those not designated, up to what it keeps) are grossed up and earn the dividend tax credit, the trust's dividends being all eligible or all not; the donation credit is allowed, with 118.1(3) D(a) for a flat-rate trust (all gifts over $200 at the highest percentage), the trust's income for the 75% limit being what it keeps with the gross-up; the 120.2 recovery of the seven preceding years' minimum tax is allowed down to the trust's minimum amount for the year, which is given (the trust's own 127.5 computation is not encoded); SIFT trusts (b) and the qualified disability trust recovery tax (c) are not encoded.
131. **The Home Buyers' Plan (146.01).** The other conditions of an eligible amount (a first-time home buyer, a qualifying home, the written agreement and acquisition dates, the specified disabled person rules) are facts. The repayment schedule starts in the first year of repayment, the year after the completion date (146.01(2)); the deferral for withdrawals in 2022 to 2025 shifts that year and is the caller's. Each year's designated repayment is given (146.01(3) requires RRSP contributions of the year or the first 60 days after, not deducted, which the caller confirms); a designation over the balance is capped at it. Rounding is down to the cent each year, and the fifteenth year takes all that remains, so nothing is lost. On emigration (146.01(5)) the balance less what is repaid within 60 days after (and before filing) is included; on death (146.01(6)) the balance less the year's designation. (4.1) and the surviving spouse's joint election to take over the balance (146.01(7)) are not encoded.
132. **The FHSA deduction (146.6(1), (5)).** Each year given is a year of the maximum participation period (from the year the first FHSA is opened; 146.6(1)(c) makes the limit nil after the period or death, so later years are not given). RRSP-to-FHSA transfers (E, F), designated amounts (C) and contributions after a first qualifying withdrawal are not modelled. The holder may deduct less than is available and carry the rest forward (146.6(5)(a)); by default all is deducted. Excess contributions are carried in B of the next year's (a), as the definition does, but the Part XI.01 tax on them (207.02) is not encoded; nor are withdrawals, transfers out, or 146.6(6) and (7).
133. **The RRSP deduction (146(1), (5), (5.1)).** The RRSP dollar limit for the year is given (it is the money purchase limit of the preceding year under 147.1(1), indexed to the average wage under the Canada Pension Plan, which Canteen has no source for); so are the preceding year's earned income, the pension adjustment and prescribed amounts, the PAR and the net PSPA, as on a notice of assessment. Earned income (146(1)) is computed for a resident from its parts; in an individual's year from employment and business income (with standby charges and option benefits), support received and paid, and business losses, rent not being separated and 8(1)(m) RPP contributions not being added back. The non-resident parts ((c), (d), (g)) are not encoded. The premiums given are those not excluded by 146(5)(a)(i) to (iv.1); own-plan and spousal-plan premiums share the limit (146(5)(b), (5.1)(b)), and a claim of less than is available is allowed, the rest carried forward. In an individual's year the deduction is a Subdivision E deduction, so it also reduces income for the minimum tax, OAS recovery and the income-tested credits. Withdrawals (146(8)) exclude HBP and LLP withdrawals; a spousal plan withdrawal is attributed to the contributor up to the contributor's spousal premiums of the year and the two before (given, net of those already attributed, 146(8.6)(a)), unless separated by breakdown, and the annuitant deducts that part (146(8.6)(b)). 146(8.7) exceptions (death, non-residence) and RRIFs (146.3(5.1)) are not encoded. The 204.2 overcontribution tax is not encoded.
